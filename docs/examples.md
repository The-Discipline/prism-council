# Example questions

## By theme
- **Pricing:** "Should I raise my day rate, or add a premium package?"
- **Offers:** "Should I turn my custom audit into a fixed-price package?"
- **Clients:** "Should I keep my biggest client, who always pays late?"
- **Acquisition:** "LinkedIn or a newsletter for the next six months?"
- **Capacity:** "Should I subcontract overflow work, or turn projects down?"

## By stage

### Starting out (no clients yet)
- "Should I quit my job now, or freelance on the side for six months first?"
- "Should I start with training or with done-for-you setup?"
- "Freelancers or small companies as my first target?"
- "€500 or €700 a day to start?"
- "Should I offer my first project at a discount in exchange for a testimonial?"

At this stage most of what you know is untested. Expect Prism to answer "Not yet" more often, with the cheapest way to test before you commit.

### First year (first clients, no rhythm yet)
- "Should I accept a long mission that blocks all prospecting, or keep shorter ones?"
- "Should I keep saying yes to everything, or start refusing projects outside my niche?"
- "Should I build a website now, or keep relying on LinkedIn and referrals?"
- "My first client wants a monthly retainer at a discount. Accept or decline?"

### Established (2 years and more, steady demand)
- "Should I raise my rate for existing clients, or only for new ones?"
- "Should I turn my most requested service into a product or a template?"
- "Should I hire a freelancer to handle delivery, or stay solo and raise prices?"
- "One client is 50% of my revenue. Diversify now, or keep riding it?"

### Changing direction
- "Should I specialize in one industry, or stay generalist?"
- "Should I drop my historical offer to focus on the one I enjoy?"
- "Should I keep freelancing, or take a full-time job at a similar income?"

## Not for Prism (and what happens instead)
- "What business could I start?" → too open. Prism asks you to name 2 or 3 options first.
- "What am I missing in my business?" → same: name options, then Prism decides between them.
- "Write my LinkedIn post" → normal writing help
- "Review my landing page copy" → normal review
- "What's the average day rate in France?" → a factual answer
- "Should I use Notion or Trello for my to-do list?" → too cheap and reversible, answered directly
